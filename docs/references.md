# References (abstract-level; full texts not read)

These four papers were found on PubMed and **only their abstracts were read**. Claims below are limited to what the abstracts state.
Official length-of-stay definitions (AIHW / METEOR, Queensland Health) are **still to be checked** (those sites could not be opened from the build environment).

| Source | What the abstract says | How it relates to this project |
|---|---|---|
| Thompson CM, et al. *Using a National Burn Registry to Develop a Model for Risk-Adjusted Length of Stay Benchmarking.* J Burn Care Res. 2023. PMID 35986490 | Builds a risk-adjusted length-of-stay (LOS) model from a national burn registry (130,729 records, 103 centres) and uses it to calculate **O/E ratios for individual centres**, to benchmark performance. Gradient-boosted regression predicted LOS better than linear regression (R2 0.67 vs 0.50). | **Supports the idea** of benchmarking centres with an observed/expected LOS ratio. **Differs in method:** their expected LOS comes from a predictive model with 22 variables; ours is a simple peer-group mean. |
| Quintairos A, et al. *A risk-adjusted length of stay to evaluate severe Community-Acquired Pneumonia (sCAP) outcomes: a machine learning analysis of 16,985 ICU admissions.* J Crit Care. 2025. PMID 40749642 | Defines a **Standardized Length of Stay Ratio (SLOSR) = sum of observed LOS / sum of predicted LOS** for ICU benchmarking. Used funnel-plot analysis; median SLOSR 1.13 (Q1 0.9, Q3 1.34). Says further research is needed in other settings. | **Matches our hospital-level definition** (total observed days / total expected days). **Differs:** predictions come from machine learning. **Related idea:** funnel plots show how uncertainty shrinks with sample size, an alternative to our planted-excess calibration. |
| Thomas JW, et al. *Interpreting risk-adjusted length of stay patterns for VA hospitals.* Med Care. 1998. PMID 9860055 | Controls for case-mix (diagnosis groups, severity, demographics) to compare hospital LOS. Reports that relative efficiency **typically varied by condition**: among 14 hospitals, none was a high outlier in all conditions, and four were high for some conditions and low for others. | **Supports the hospital x diagnosis view** (chart 2): a hospital can look fine overall yet differ by condition. |
| Singh D, Cameron K. *Association Between Discharge Destination and Excess Length of Stay in Heart Failure-Related Admissions.* J Eval Clin Pract. 2026. PMID 42623497 | Defines **excess LOS as max(LOS - CMS geometric mean LOS, 0)** against a diagnosis-related benchmark. In 1,076 admissions at one hospital, excess stay was **not associated with lower 30-day readmission**. Single site, observational. | **Related definition** of excess stay against a diagnosis benchmark (they use a geometric mean and count only excess). Their readmission finding is a different question from ours, so it is context, not confirmation. |

## What this means for our method (decision rule from `docs/TODO.md`)
- **Concept confirmed:** comparing observed with expected LOS as a ratio, summed to centre level, is used in the literature.
- **Our method is a simplified version:** peer-group mean (diagnosis group x age band) instead of a model that adjusts for severity and comorbidity. This stays, and is documented as a simplification.
- **Possible future refinements (not made):** model-based expected LOS; the **geometric mean** for skewed LOS (used by the CMS benchmark in the 2026 paper); funnel plots.
- **Still open:** the official Australian definition of LOS and how same-day stays are counted. Until it is read, the "same-day counts as 1 day" convention stays marked "verify before quoting".
